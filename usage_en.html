How to use Sand Player

Sand Player home / 日本語

Sand Player puts two videos or images side by side, with a difference view and a blend (overlay) view in between, so you can find what changed. The red boxes and numbers in the images match the numbers in the text.

1. Load videos

  • Drop onto the left half (L) of the window to load into L, or the right half (R) to load into R
  • Drop as many as you like; they are added to that side's playlist in order. Drop a folder to load its subfolders too
  • Files opened with Sand Player from File Explorer (MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM) go into L
  • Playback starts automatically once both L and R have a video

Supported formats

TypeFormats
VideoMP4, MOV, MKV, WebM (H.264, H.265/HEVC, VP8, VP9, AV1)
ImagesPNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, BMP, AVIF

Editing codecs such as ProRes and DNxHD, AVI, MTS, WMV, FLV, MXF, and TIFF images are not supported. A video that can't be played shows "This video can't be played" in its frame. Playing H.265 requires a GPU with hardware H.265 decoding.

2. Three views

Difference

Shows the difference between L and R. Matching areas are black and only the differences light up, so you can spot any difference right away.

Blend

Overlays R on L, semi-transparent (1). Change R's opacity with the slider at the bottom of the middle view (2) — it starts at 50%.

Both

Switch views with "Display mode" in the gear menu.

  • Even during playback, frames with the same timestamp are compared. Put the same video on both sides and the difference stays black
  • When lengths differ, the shorter video holds on its last frame while the comparison continues

Step one frame with ← / → or the mouse wheel, 1/3 second with Shift, and 1 second with Alt.

Seek bar handles and markers

  • Hover over a handle on the seek bar (position, A, or B) to show a "marker" right above it, with the time in seconds and the frame number
  • You can also drag the marker to move the handle. The marker stays visible while you drag
  • Hold Shift while dragging to snap to a fixed step counted from frame 0 (with 10 frames: frame 0, 10, 20, ...). Set the step in frames or seconds with "Shift + drag step" in the gear menu
  • While you step frames with ← / → or the mouse wheel, and for about 2 seconds after, the position marker is shown

When L and R have different frame rates, a warning appears at the top of the window. In that case, the sync between them is slightly off.

  • If your PC can't keep up (for example, with high-resolution video) and a video can't be shown at its original frame rate, a warning appears at the top: "Playback is too heavy to show the original frame rate" (when it stays below 90% for 3 seconds; it disappears once playback recovers). Showing only one of difference or blend, or using a smaller window, helps

4. Use the playlists

  • Open: move the mouse to the left or right edge of the window; it closes when you move away. Press L or the Playlist button to keep it open
  • Switch: W / S for the previous / next video in L, ↑ / ↓ for R, and PageUp / PageDown for both. Clicking an item also switches. Both sides restart from the beginning when you switch
  • Reorder: drag items. Drag to the other playlist to move them between L and R
  • Insert: drop files between playlist items to add them there
  • Select several (1): Ctrl + click to add items one by one, Shift + click for a range. Drag selected items together. Press Esc to clear the selection
  • Remove (2): drop items on "Drag here to remove from list" at the bottom, or select them and press Delete

5. Show captions

Press C or the CC button to cycle through:

ModeShows
CC (off)Nothing
CC₁ (detail)File name, resolution, frame rate, codec, duration, total frames, current time and frame number
CC₂ (name only)The file name, large in the middle
CC₃ (name and frame)The file name and the current frame number, in one small line in a corner
  • Frame numbers count from "Start frame" in the gear menu (0 by default; set it to 1, 1001, or whatever your studio uses)
  • Move captions to any corner by dragging them, with Ctrl + arrow keys, or with "Caption position" in the gear menu
  • Change their size with "Caption size" in the gear menu

6. Right-click menu and single view

The top of the menu says "Left view settings" or "Right view settings", so you know which side you are changing. The playlists stay hidden while the menu is open.

7. Time shift (offset playback timing)

You can shift when L and R each start playing. Use it to compare the same scenes in two clips whose start points differ, or footage that moved earlier or later in the edit. Right-click L or R and enter a value under "Time shift".

No.ItemWhat it does
1secThe shift in seconds. + starts later, − starts earlier
2framesThe shift in frames. Enter either one and the other updates automatically (snapped to that video's frames)
3RangeHow far you can shift: earlier, until that side's last frame reaches frame 0; later, up to the length of the longer of L and R
4Reset time shiftSet this side's shift back to 0
5Reset time shift on both sidesSet the shift of both L and R back to 0
  • Not started yet (1): a side shifted later holds its first frame, dimmed, until it starts
  • Shifted sides (2, 3): a label such as "⇄ +48 f" above the view shows the shift. Click it to open a time shift window over both L and R, with a slider as well as the input fields (the slider covers the full range, earlier and later, and a white tick marks 0, no shift; hold Shift while sliding to snap to a fixed step counted from 0)
  • Original frame 0 (4): while a side is shifted, a blue line on the seek bar marks frame 0 of the unshifted video. If a side is shifted earlier, the seek bar starts before 0 (for example -0:01)
  • Difference and blend take the shift into account and compare frames from the same moment
  • The shift belongs to L or R, not to a video: it stays when you switch to another video in that side's playlist
  • If switching videos changes the range so that a shift is out of range, the message "The time shift limit changed, so it was adjusted automatically" appears and the shift is brought back into range (for example, a side shifted 100 frames earlier switched to a 3-second video can only stay 3 seconds earlier)
  • Time shifts go back to 0 when you close the app

8. HDR video

For comparison, HDR (HLG / PQ) video is shown as its signal values without tone mapping, not with its intended HDR look. This way L, R, difference, and blend all show the same colors. Portrait HDR video from iPhone is supported.
